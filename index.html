Your accuracy by encoding

perceptron

Each round shows one value drawn as a single visual characteristic, called an encoding. Read it, then place your guess in one click (or, on a phone, drag and lock in). Your accuracy is scored and tracked per encoding.

The encodings are ordered by the Cleveland–McGill graphical-perception hierarchy — a classic finding that people read some encodings far more accurately than others. Position along a common scale is read best; area and colour are read worst. Playing is a hands-on demo of that ranking.

Anonymous results from your rounds (the encoding, your guess, the target, and a random session id) are recorded to study how people read charts. No personal data is collected.
